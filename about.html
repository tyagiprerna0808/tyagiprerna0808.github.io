three lanes, one discipline

statistics is the core, not the garnish

Isolation forests, PD curves, embedding distances and attention weights are all the same subject wearing different clothes. When a detection rule needs a threshold, that’s a question about distributions. When an LLM eval disagrees with itself, that’s a question about variance. I trust people who can derive the thing they’re deploying, so I try to be one.

ml with real stakes plays by different rules

When a model decides whose account gets frozen, you don’t get to pick a comfortable operating point. A false positive is a real person locked out of their own salary; a false negative is someone’s savings gone. The unglamorous middle is where the real work lives: feature engineering, threshold tuning against costs that aren’t symmetric, and being able to explain exactly why the model flagged the account it flagged.

genai and agents are systems, not demos

An agent that produces a beautiful answer once is a demo. An agent that produces an auditable, version-controlled result on a schedule, reliably, is a system, and the gap between the two is where the interesting work lives. Orchestration, retrieval quality, evals, guardrails, lineage. The model itself is maybe 20% of it.
